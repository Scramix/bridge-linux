# Bridge Linux Code of Conduct

Bridge Linux is an open-source project, and that means people from different backgrounds, skill levels, and experience are going to work on it.

That is a good thing. People are going to disagree sometimes, especially when it comes to technical decisions. The goal of this Code of Conduct is to make sure those disagreements stay productive and do not turn into people attacking each other.

This Code of Conduct applies to the Bridge Linux GitHub repository and other spaces officially used by the Bridge Linux project.

## The basic rules

* **Be friendly and patient.**
* **Be respectful.** You do not have to agree with someone to treat them with respect.
* **Be welcoming.** Beginners are welcome here. Nobody should be made fun of for not knowing something.
* **Be considerate.** Remember that other people may have different experience levels, opinions, cultures, and ways of communicating.
* **Do not harass people.**
* **Do not make threats or encourage violence.**
* **Do not post sexually explicit material or excessively violent material.**
* **Do not post someone else's private or identifying information.**
* **Do not use discriminatory insults, jokes, or language against other people.**
* **Do not repeatedly bother someone after they have asked you to stop.**
* **Do not deliberately disrupt the project or its community.**

## Technical disagreements

Technical disagreements are completely normal.

You can say that you think someone's code is wrong. You can explain why you think a design is a bad idea. You can propose a completely different approach.

What you cannot do is turn a technical disagreement into a personal attack.

Focus on the code, the design, or the problem being discussed, not on attacking the person who made it.

## If you make a mistake

Everyone makes mistakes. That includes contributors, maintainers, and me.

If you make a mistake, you are expected to fix it when reasonably possible. Other people should also give you a reasonable opportunity to correct mistakes instead of immediately attacking you for them.

## Reporting problems

If you see someone violating this Code of Conduct, please report it through a private GitHub communication with the Bridge Linux project maintainer rather than starting a public argument.

If you are unsure whether something violates this Code of Conduct, you can still report it. It is better to ask than to let a serious problem continue.

Reports will be handled as fairly and privately as reasonably possible.

## Enforcement

Depending on what happened, actions may include:

* Asking someone to stop the behavior.
* Removing inappropriate comments or content.
* Temporarily restricting someone's participation.
* Permanently banning someone from participating in the project.

The response will depend on the circumstances and severity of the situation.

The purpose of enforcement is not to punish people for making ordinary mistakes or having different technical opinions. The purpose is to keep Bridge Linux a place where people can actually work together.

## Questions

If you have a question about this Code of Conduct, you can open a GitHub issue or discussion about it.

This Code of Conduct may be updated as the Bridge Linux community grows and new situations come up.
